QuickTag Privacy Policy

QuickTag – SEO Meta Tag Checker, for Chrome and Firefox · Effective 21 October 2026

In short: QuickTag doesn't collect, sell or share any personal data. It reads the page you're viewing inside your browser to show its SEO tags, and nothing you view is sent anywhere.

What QuickTag is

QuickTag is a browser extension that shows a web page's SEO tags in a panel on the page, so you can check them and copy them into a spreadsheet. This policy explains what QuickTag reads, what it saves, and what it does with that information.

What QuickTag reads

On websites where you switch QuickTag on, it reads these parts of the page you're viewing so it can show them in the panel:

  • the page address (URL);
  • the title tag;
  • meta tags: description, robots, googlebot, viewport, Open Graph and Twitter Card tags;
  • canonical and hreflang link tags;
  • H1 to H6 headings, and whether each one is visible;
  • the page's language and character set;
  • structured data (JSON-LD and microdata types);
  • the web app manifest and icon link tags (manifest, icon and apple-touch-icon).

This all happens inside your browser. QuickTag doesn't read form fields, passwords, cookies or your browsing history. On websites where QuickTag is switched off, its script only checks whether you've switched it on for that site, and reads nothing from the page.

What QuickTag saves

QuickTag saves a small amount of information in your browser's extension storage, on your device:

  • the names of the websites (for example, www.example.com) you've switched QuickTag on for;
  • your panel preferences: which side it sits on, whether it's collapsed, and which sections are open;
  • your default copy format.

Your keyboard shortcut is saved by your browser. None of this is synced to an account or sent anywhere, and it's all deleted when you remove QuickTag.

Your clipboard

QuickTag writes to your clipboard only when you click a Copy button or copy icon. It never reads your clipboard.

What QuickTag sends

Nothing. QuickTag has no servers, accounts, analytics, advertising or tracking, and it doesn't use any third-party services or load code from the internet.

One thing to be aware of: when you open the Social tags section, QuickTag shows a preview of the page's og:image or twitter:image. Your browser loads that image directly from the address the page lists, just as it loads any other image on the page. QuickTag adds no information to that request and sends no referrer.

To check that a site's icons are the right size, QuickTag downloads the site's web app manifest and the icon files that the page and the manifest list, from the addresses they give, much as your browser loads them itself. While the Manifest & icons section is open, it also checks the usual addresses on the site you're viewing (/site.webmanifest, /manifest.json, /manifest.webmanifest and /favicon.ico) when the page doesn't link those files. QuickTag adds no information to these requests, and what comes back is only shown in the panel.

Sharing and selling

QuickTag doesn't sell, rent, share or transfer any data. It doesn't use data for advertising, for credit or lending decisions, or for anything unrelated to showing you a page's SEO tags. QuickTag's use of information complies with the Chrome Web Store User Data Policy, including the Limited Use requirements, and with Mozilla's Add-on Policies.

Your choices

  • Switch QuickTag off for a site at any time with the keyboard shortcut or the × button in the panel.
  • Remove QuickTag from your browser to delete everything it has saved.
